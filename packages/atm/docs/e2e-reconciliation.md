# End-to-end reconciliation matrix

Does the money the reference handler books agree with what hledger reports, with what Stripe says, and with arithmetic done by hand? This is the answer for every handler path that moves money, case by case, where it does not, and why. Written for someone who has not read the code. Related: `docs/financial-reports.md` (what exists and what is missing), `docs/log-and-r2.md` (the shipper), `docs/README.md`.

The matrix is a test, not a report: `tests/e2e-reconcile.test.ts` runs it, the cases are scenario bundles in `fixtures/scenarios/<id>/` (plain data, see `scenario-bundles.md`; the TypeScript literals they were converted from are gone, and the comments that worked the numbers out are in each `expected.json` as `notes`), and `tests/golden/e2e/<case>/` holds what each case printed. `UPDATE_GOLDEN=1 bun test tests/e2e-reconcile.test.ts` rewrites the goldens after a deliberate change. It needs hledger (1.52.1 is what the goldens were made with) and skips without it.

## What each case does

Each case delivers signed Stripe events to the real handler (`handleStripeWebhook`, an in-memory SQLite), then follows the money three ways.

1. **The handler's own books.** The outbox goes through `tfd ship` into a folder, `tfd journal` and `tfd report` (`reportMonth`: income statement, balance sheet, credits) make the journals and hledger prints the reports, and the refunds and disputes registers come from `hledger reg`. Checked: `hledger check balanced accounts commodities` passes; every credit commodity sums to zero over the whole book; the credit balances of the replayed log (`replayEvents`) are the credit register's `credits:avail:*` and `credits:held:*`.
2. **`tfd reconcile` against a Stripe side built by hand.** Stripe's invoices, refunds (with their own `re_` ids, charge and payment intent), payouts, disputes and balance transactions (amount, fee, net) are written out in the case, in Stripe's documented shapes, per month. They are never derived from the events. The shipped folder and the Stripe files go through `reconcileFolder`, the code behind `tfd reconcile --root --school --month --stripe`. As a check on the oracle itself, the test also asserts that the case's Stripe balance transactions add up to the hand numbers (Stripe's balance, fees, refunds).
3. **Hand numbers.** Every case lists, in its body, what the books must say: income by account, refunds, disputes, fees, dispute fees, `assets:stripe`, the payout clearing account, net income and credit balances, with the arithmetic in a comment (for example `260.00 - 50.00 - 7.84 = 202.16`, or for a lost dispute `260.00 - 260.00 - 7.84 - 15.00 = -22.84`). A case that crosses a month also has the hand numbers of each month's column of the monthly income statement. Goldens are the output of the code, so they are not trusted on their own: the hand numbers are asserted first.

A case with a defect (`DISCREPANCY`) asserts today's behaviour: its `pinned` numbers must be exactly what the books say, and must differ from the hand numbers (so a fix makes the test fail, and the failure says which pin is stale); its `reconcile` list names the differences `tfd reconcile` must print. The ids a bundle pins are in its `expected.json` (`discrepancies`, with `notes` saying where). (The harness also has a `KNOWN` verdict for a defect another branch is fixing; no case has it now, see K1 and K2.) A book whose log leaves out money on purpose, a non-USD invoice, says so with `skipped`: `reconcile`'s own `skipped` count must be exactly that, and the differences it lists are the ones the count explains, so the case is not a discrepancy. No case is skipped (the file as a whole skips only when hledger is not installed, like the other hledger tests). The matrix itself changed nothing in `src/`; the fix of D4 (below) is the first change a finding made.

Each case directory holds: `events.csv` (the handler's decisions, one line each: date, type, idem, postings), `income-statement.csv`, `balance-sheet.csv`, `credits.csv`, `refunds-register.csv` and `disputes-register.csv` (only when not empty), `income-statement-monthly.csv` and `balance-sheet-monthly.csv` (cases that cross a month) and `reconcile.txt` (`tfd reconcile`'s own text for each month). `tests/golden/e2e/matrix.csv` is the table below in cents-and-dollars, hand beside report, one row per book.

D3, a payout that fails after `payout.paid`, and D4, a refused invoice that left the books short, are fixed (below): `payout-failed` and `refused-invoice` are OK. No case is KNOWN any more. ### D1: no fee unless the caller passes one

- **Case:** `fee-unknown`.
- **Where:** `src/billing/money.ts:132` (`fee ?? 0`) and `src/handler/stripe-webhook.ts:578` (`feeOf`: no `paymentFee` dependency, no fee). Documented in `docs/financial-reports.md` ("The fee is the caller's").
- **Why:** no webhook event carries Stripe's fee; it is on the charge's balance transaction, which the family service has to read first. Without it the payment is booked gross (`fee_known: false`) and a fee learned later is not booked.
- **Impact:** about 3% of every payment (7.84 on 260.00). Net income is 7.84 too high per payment, and `assets:stripe` can never reach zero after a payout: it keeps the fees for ever (7.84 in the case). `tfd reconcile` notices (`not_in_log fees`).
- **Status (Unreleased): the books are unchanged and no longer silent. Still open: the fee itself.** The case is still DISCREPANCY with the same hand numbers and pins (`fees` 7.84 / 0.00, `assets:stripe` 0.00 / 7.84, net 252.16 / 260.00); what changed is what says so. See "What the handler says now" and "Booking a late fee: a proposal, not built" below.

#### What the handler says now (built)

- **The answer.** An `invoice.paid` whose payment the books hold at the gross amount (no `paymentFee` dependency, or it answered `null`/`undefined`) is still `200 applied`, and `body.feeUnknown` lists it, one `<invoice id> <amount paid> cents` per payment (`26000` is 260.00: `in_1 26000 cents`). The key is absent when every payment has its fee, so an answer with a fee is byte for byte what it was.
- **The warning.** `deps.warn` (default stderr) gets one line per such delivery: `atm: fee_unknown: <event id> (invoice.paid) was applied, but 1 payment(s) are booked at the gross amount with no Stripe fee (no paymentFee dependency is configured | paymentFee did not know the fee | on a re-send, paymentFee knows the fee now, but the payment was booked by an earlier delivery without it): in_1 26000 cents. The fee is not booked later: ...`. The two causes are told apart because they have different cures (wire the hook; or make the family service answer 5xx until the balance transaction is readable).
- **A fee known later.** A re-send of the same invoice under another Stripe event id (the dashboard's re-send after the balance transaction became readable) decides no event at all (the invoice is already applied), so the fee would be lost without a word. The handler now looks at the payment the outbox holds for the invoice (`stripe:<invoice id>`): when it has `fee_known: false`, the answer lists it again, with ` (fee 784 known now, not booked: the first delivery booked the payment without it)` when the re-send's `paymentFee` knew one, and the handler warns. Nothing is booked. The same holds for the payment of a refused paid invoice (#87): the retry that applies finds the payment the refusal booked, whose fee is the refusal's, and says so.
- **The refused answer.** The 422 note for a refused paid invoice whose booked payment has no fee says so too (`It is booked at the gross amount, without a Stripe fee (fee_known false), and the fee is not booked later.`).
- **The outbox.** The event already says it: `payload.fee_known: false` and `fee: 0` on the `payment.succeeded`. Nothing was added to the event on purpose: its bytes feed the replay goldens and every reader.
- **reconcile.** `ReconcileResult.feeUnknown` counts the `payment.succeeded` events dated in the period with `fee_known === false` (an event with no `fee_known` is not counted); the text prints `no fee booked: N payment(s) with fee_known false (their fee is not in the log)` when N > 0; and a `fees` difference (`not_in_log` or `amount_differs`) ends `; N payment(s) in the log have fee_known false: their fee was never booked`, so the cause is in the line. In the matrix only `fee-unknown/reconcile.txt` changed (a new line after the header and the sentence on the difference); every other golden is the same.
- **tfd report.** `ReportRun.feeUnknown` (`inMonth`, `total`) and `feeUnknownNote` give a second stderr line, as the non-USD one does: `atm report: 1 payment(s) in 2026-01 were booked without their Stripe fee (1 in all events); expenses:billing:stripe-fees and net income are off by those fees, and assets:stripe keeps them after a payout`. stdout is still only hledger's text, and the journal and the reports are unchanged.
- **Tests.** `tests/handler-fee-unknown.test.ts`, through the real handler with hand numbers (2.9% + 30 cents on 260.00 is 7.54 + 0.30 = 7.84, net 252.16): no hook, hook `null`, hook present (answer and postings unchanged: 252.16 / 7.84 / -260.00), the same Stripe event again (`duplicate`, no second warning), a fee known only on a re-send, a re-send of a payment that has its fee, a payout before the payment, `reconcile` (count, text, cause in the fees difference, another month 0, no `fee_known` not counted), and hledger (`check balanced accounts commodities` passes; after a 252.16 payout `assets:stripe` holds 7.84 and there is no expense: the cost of the missing fee) with `tfd report`'s stderr. The matrix asserts `fee-unknown` warns once with `fee_unknown` and no other case does.

#### Which Stripe events carry the fee: none (the evidence)

B (booking a late fee from an event) needs an event that carries a payment's fee. The fixtures, searched for `fee`, `net` and `balance_transaction`, say there is none the handler receives:

|---|---|---|

| `charge.refunded` (`recorded/charge.refunded.json`, `delivered/*/04-charge.refunded.json`, `recorded/booking/charge.refunded.*.json`) | the charge's own, a bare id (`txn_3UYLWhScrubTest01gKLXPIa`) | no: the id is not expanded, and a `Charge` has no `fee` field; the amount needs `GET /v1/balance_transactions/<id>` |
| `payout.paid` (`payout.paid.json`) | the payout's own, a bare id (`txn_SYNTH_0009`); `failure_balance_transaction` null | no: `amount` is the net of everything the payout carried (24500 in the fixture), with no list of the charges, refunds or fees in it |
| `charge.dispute.created`, `.closed` (`delivered/dispute-*/`) | expanded, with `fee` 1500 and `fee_details` "Dispute fee" | a dispute's fee, not a payment's: #66 already books it (`expenses:billing:dispute-fees`) |

So the only readers of a payment's fee are Stripe's API (the balance transaction of the charge) and the caller's `paymentFee` hook, which is the contract the family service already has. No webhook can settle it, and nor can `payout.paid`: a payout's amount is a sum of nets, so a book whose only payment has no fee shows the fee as the 7.84 left in `assets:stripe` after the payout, but with two payments, a refund or a payout that carries earlier money, that residue cannot be split by payment, and inventing it would put an inferred number in the books as if Stripe had said it. `tfd reconcile` against Stripe's own balance transactions is the check that finds the amount (`stripe 7.84`), and it now says why.

#### Booking a late fee: a proposal, not built

Why not built: every way to book a fee after its payment needs a posting that no existing event type makes. `payment.succeeded` is the only event with a fee line and its idem (`stripe:<invoice id>`) is exactly what makes a re-send harmless (the outbox keeps the first row), so a second `payment.succeeded` is either dropped or, under another idem, books the 260.00 income twice. `payment.disputed` books a different account (`dispute-fees`) and a withdrawal. A new type is a closed-enum change (#68): `AtmEventType`, `schema/envelope.v1.json`, a payload schema, `moneyPostings`, `validateEvent`, replay and the journal all learn it, and a reader older than the change (an `atm journal`, `atm report` or `reconcile` of an earlier version run on a shipped folder that has the new event) refuses the log. That is the owner's call, not a silent one. The design, if the owner wants it:

- **Event.** `payment.fee_adjusted`, payload `{ payment_id, fee, amount_paid, currency }`, idem `stripe:<payment id>:fee`, dated by the payment's own date or by the moment the fee was read (the owner chooses: the first keeps a closed month closed, the second changes the month it is booked in).
- **Postings.** `expenses:billing:stripe-fees` +fee, `assets:stripe` -fee (two lines, sums to zero, USD only). 260.00 with a fee of 7.84: before, `assets:stripe` 260.00 and no expense; after, `assets:stripe` 252.16, fees 7.84, net income 252.16, and the 252.16 payout takes `assets:stripe` to 0.00. This is additive and commutative with the payment, so an adjustment that arrives before its payment (out of order) still ends at the same totals, as a payout before its payment does.
- **Guard.** It must be emitted only for a payment held with `fee_known: false`, or the fee is booked twice (a payment that has its fee plus an adjustment). The handler can check that (the same outbox read as `feeUnknownLines`); a caller that writes it itself (the way it writes `share.settled`) has to. The fee is a whole number of cents from 0 to the amount paid, the same `feeProblem` rule, refused otherwise. The idem makes a redelivery book once; a different fee for the same payment is a conflict the outbox does not show (it keeps the first), so the first adjustment wins, as the first payment does.
- **Trigger.** There is no event to trigger it (see the evidence), so it is a command: a re-send of `invoice.paid` whose `paymentFee` now knows the fee (the handler finds the held payment with `fee_known: false` and books the adjustment), or a function the family service calls with the fee it read. Stripe never retries a 200, so the first delivery's `applied` is final unless somebody re-sends.
- **Consequences for the readers.** `reconcile` needs no change (it sums the postings to the two fee accounts and `assets:stripe`), the report and the journal take the new type from the postings, replay and the goldens of every existing log are unchanged, and `fee_known` on the original payment stays `false` (the log is append-only; the adjustment is the correction).

Options for the owner, with what each costs:

1. **Leave it as built here** (loud, books unchanged). Cost: the fee of every payment without one stays out of the books until somebody enters it by hand, a month already reported does not change, and a deployer must wire `paymentFee` and have the family service answer 5xx until the balance transaction is readable.
2. **Add `payment.fee_adjusted`** as above. Cost: a new event type, a minimum reader version before the first one is written, and a decision on the date.
3. **Make the fee required, opt in** (no new event type): a `WebhookDeps` switch under which an `invoice.paid` with no readable fee is answered 5xx and nothing is written (the claim released), so Stripe retries for up to three days until the fee is readable and the payment is never booked gross. Cost: the family's credits wait for the fee (an asynchronous payment method can take days), a new answer value (`WebhookOutcome` is a union too), and a deployment without the hook cannot take payments.

### D2: `charge.refunded` without `previous_attributes` books the running total

- **Case:** `refund-no-previous` (a refund of 50.00 and one of 30.00, whose events say 50.00 and 80.00 refunded in all).
- **Where:** `src/billing/money.ts:209` (`refund.amount - (refund.previousAmount ?? 0)`).
- **Why:** the amount of one refund is the charge's refunded total less what it was before the event; with no `previous_attributes` the "before" is taken as 0. Documented as "Not built" in `docs/financial-reports.md`.
- **Impact:** the second refund is booked at 80.00 instead of 30.00: refunds 50.00 too high, `assets:stripe` and net income 50.00 too low in the case. Latent: every recorded `charge.refunded` carries `previous_attributes`; whether live Stripe ever omits it is not known. `tfd reconcile` notices (`amount_differs refund`, `amount_differs net`).
- **Fix:** keep the refunded total per charge in the holder's grants row and book the difference, or refuse (422 `bad_event`) a `charge.refunded` that carries no `previous_attributes` when the charge has had a refund.

### D3: a payout that fails after `payout.paid` was not reversed (fixed)

- **Case:** `payout-failed` (a payment of 260.00 with a fee of 7.84, a payout of 252.16 on the 17th, the payout failing on the 19th).
- **What it was:** `payout.failed` was not in `HANDLED_TYPES`: answered `200 ignored`, nothing recorded. Only `payout.paid` was mapped. The payout stayed in `assets:clearing:stripe-payouts` as if it had reached the bank (252.16 there, `assets:stripe` 0.00 instead of 252.16, so 252.16 too low), and a payout Stripe then made again would have been booked a second time. `tfd reconcile` noticed (`missing_in_stripe payout po_1`).
- **What the books should say.** A failed payout puts its money back in the Stripe balance, so the clearing account goes down and `assets:stripe` up by the payout's amount, and nothing else moves: the fee was Stripe's from the start, and income was never the payout's. In the case: `assets:stripe` 252.16, clearing 0.00, fees 7.84, net income 252.16.
- **The fix (#104):** `payout.failed` is mapped (`schema/mapping/stripe.payout.failed.v1.json`, the same `StripePayout` fact as `payout.paid`) and books a `payout.paid` event with its sides swapped: `assets:stripe` +252.16, `assets:clearing:stripe-payouts` -252.16, payload `{ id, amount, currency, failed: true }`, idem `stripe:<payout id>:failed`, dated by the failure event (19 January), not by the payout. No new event type (a closed list rejects one in a reader built before it, as with `money.skipped` and the share events); the payload schema only gains an optional `failed`.
- **Out of order, or never paid.** The reversal takes the payout out of the clearing account, so it must find the payout there. A `payout.failed` whose `payout.paid` is not booked (looked up by the idem `stripe:<payout id>` in the outbox) is answered 409 `unmatched_payment` with no claim, as a refund before its link is: Stripe retries it, and the retry after the `payout.paid` reverses it. Booked blind, it would leave the clearing account negative and `assets:stripe` too high by the payout. Stripe also fails a payout that was never `paid`; that one has nothing to reverse (the Trifold engine only moves the cash at `payout.paid`), so it stays 409 until Stripe stops retrying, up to three days, and books nothing. The price of that choice is those answers in the Stripe dashboard's failing-deliveries list.
- **Redelivery, other currencies, a second payout.** The idem is per payout, so the same failure under a new Stripe event id books once (the outbox keeps the first). A `payout.paid` delivered again after the failure books nothing. A payout in another currency is answered `non_usd` with the payout's one marker (idem `stripe:<id>:non_usd`; a failure adds none) and never waits. A payout Stripe pays out again is a new `po_` id and books as any payout: after paid, failed and paid again the clearing account holds only the new one.
- **reconcile.** Stripe lists the failed payout with status `failed`, which is not compared (`payout_not_paid`). The log's side now leaves out a payout the log reversed, both its lines, in whichever month each falls, and counts it in neither side, so it is no difference. A payout the log reversed that Stripe still lists as `paid` is `missing_in_log`; one the log has as paid that Stripe lists as failed is still `missing_in_stripe` (the failure is not booked yet).
- **The oracle:** the hand numbers of the case did not change (`assets:stripe` 252.16, clearing 0.00, fees 7.84, net 252.16); the old `pinned` pair and the one reconcile difference were removed. They were written down before the first run on the fixed code, with the golden changes: one more line in `events.csv` (19 January, `stripe:po_1:failed`), the balance sheet and income statement dated to the 19th, `assets:stripe` 252.16 where the clearing account stood, and `reconcile.txt` ending `no differences` with `payout: Stripe 0, log 0`. The golden diff was exactly that and nothing else.
- **Options not taken.** (a) A new event type `payout.failed`: the cleanest name, but the envelope's `event_type` list is closed, so every reader built before it (`validateEvent`, DuckDB over the envelope, an older `atm journal`) rejects a log that holds one, and the owner would have to upgrade readers before the first failure. Reusing `payout.paid` costs a misleading name instead (a reader that counts `payout.paid` events as payouts counts the failure too; `failed: true` marks it) and nothing else. (b) A negative `payout.paid` amount: the payload schema's `minimum: 1` would reject it in an older reader. (c) Booking a failure that arrives before its payout, or for a payout never paid: it leaves the clearing account negative and `assets:stripe` too high; remembering the failure so a later `payout.paid` is not booked needs state the outbox does not have. (d) Answering such a failure 200 `ignored`: it cannot tell a late `payout.paid` from a payout never paid, and a 200 would lose the real reversal.
- **Not covered:** `payout.canceled` (a payout canceled before it is paid; the Trifold engine books nothing for it and it is `ignored`), a payout Stripe reverses after it has been paid for good, and a failure of an instant or a Connect payout.

### D4: a paid invoice the handler refuses was not in the books until a human fixed it (fixed)

- **Case:** `refused-invoice` (an invoice line whose price the menu does not know: `422 refused unmatched_line`; the case delivers it twice, as Stripe's retry does).
- **What it was:** `src/billing/handle.ts` refused the whole invoice, the handler released the claim and wrote nothing, so no `payment.succeeded` was booked although `invoice.paid` means Stripe has collected the money. The family had paid and had no credits, and the books lacked the whole payment (260.00 income, 7.84 fee, 252.16 cash in the case) until somebody fixed the menu and the event was sent again (Stripe retries a 422 for up to three days; after that only a re-send does). `tfd reconcile` noticed (`missing_in_log invoice`, plus the fee and net totals).
- **The fix (#87):** a paid invoice the handler refuses after the claim, for a reason that leaves the money certain, now books its `payment.succeeded` (or, for a non-USD invoice, the `money.skipped` marker) in the same transaction that releases the claim. The answer is still `422 refused` and the claim is still released: Stripe keeps retrying, the credits are still not granted, the invoice is still not indexed. Nothing else is written. Full reasoning, the cases and what was rejected: `docs/financial-reports.md`, "A paid invoice the handler refuses".
- **What the retry does:** the payment line's idem is `stripe:<invoice id>` (it always was), and the outbox keeps the first row of an idem (`ON CONFLICT (school_id, env, idem) DO NOTHING`). So Stripe's own retries of the refused event, and the delivery that finally applies after the menu is fixed, make the same event and the outbox keeps the one it has: the income is booked once. The applied delivery also grants the credits and indexes the invoice, as it always did.
- **Which refusals:** `unmatched_line`, `tier_on_other_subscription`, `addon_only_subscription`, `rejected` (all four only for an `invoice.paid`). Every refusal before the claim (bad signature, bad body, wrong `livemode`, another school, half a holder, another product's invoice) and the ones where the amount or the holder cannot be trusted after it (`unknown_mapping`, `bad_event`, `missing_metadata`, `unknown_school`) book nothing, as before. The table is in `docs/financial-reports.md`.
- **Unchanged and now visible in the same book:** a refund or dispute of a refused-and-booked invoice still waits (409 `unmatched_payment`), and its `invoice_payment.paid` too (409 `unmatched_invoice`), until the invoice is applied: the credits it must take back do not exist before that. `tfd reconcile` then shows a `missing_in_log refund`, no longer an invoice and a fee. For an invoice that stays refused for good (a second subscription for a member whose first ended, or `tier_on_other_subscription` when the member is refunded instead of fixed) the refund cannot be booked by the handler and has to be entered by hand; the income stays booked.
- **The oracle:** the hand numbers of the case did not change (260.00 income under `income:billing:other`, fees 7.84, `assets:stripe` 252.16, net 252.16, no visits); the old `pinned` zeros and the three reconcile differences were removed, and the first run on the fixed code agreed with the hand numbers before the golden was written. The one thing the hand numbers did not predict is the file `credits.csv`, which now exists with only its `Total: 0` line (a book with a money line and no credit posting).

### D5: reconcile on a shared Stripe account reports another product's invoices as missing

- **Case:** `foreign-invoice` (a 50.00 invoice with no `b4u_*` metadata: `200 ignored`, correctly not booked).
- **Where:** `src/ledger/reconcile.ts:512` pairs every Stripe invoice with the log; a Stripe invoice record does not carry the metadata that says whose it is.
- **Impact:** a false alarm per payment of another product (3 differences for one invoice, no money wrong). Documented in the handler's header ("keep the families on their own account").
- **Fix:** let the invoice record carry its metadata and reconcile only the school's invoices, or keep the documented rule of one Stripe account per school.

### D6: one payment, one income account

- **Case:** `create-with-addon` (a tier of 260.00 and a recurring add-on of 50.00 on one invoice).
- **Where:** `src/billing/money.ts:85-103` (`incomeKindOf`: "an invoice that pays for both books all of it under the tier").
- **Why:** a `payment.succeeded` has one income posting, named after one product.
- **Impact:** none on net income, cash or fees. Revenue by product is wrong: 50.00 of add-on revenue is booked as membership on that invoice and on every renewal that carries the add-on, so `income:billing:addon:*` is understated and `income:billing:membership:*` overstated by the same amount. The totals and reconcile agree (no difference), which is why only the per-account hand numbers show it.
- **Fix:** one income posting per resolved invoice line group (the tier, each add-on), with an invoice-level discount shared in proportion.

### D7: a renewal delivered before its create invoice takes the create invoice's place

- **Case:** `renewal-before-create` (a bundle outside the matrix: `fixtures/scenarios/renewal-before-create/`).
- **Where:** `src/billing/grants.ts` (the first invoice of a subscription names its anchor and cycle); `docs/stripe-findings.md` already lists it as not fixed.
- **Why:** Stripe promises no order. The renewal arrives first, is answered 200 `applied` and becomes the holder's subscription; the create invoice then answers 422 `refused` `rejected` on every retry, and its `invoice_payment.paid` link answers 409 `unmatched_invoice` for good.
- **Impact:** money is right (a refused paid invoice still books its payment: income 520.00, fees 15.68, net 504.32, reconcile clean), credits are wrong: the holder has 2 visits where the in-order story gives 4, because the create invoice's grant is never made.
- **Related:** `tests/scenario-order.test.ts` shuffles every bundle whose deliveries are all applied and checks that H4 to H8 do not change; eight bundles (renewal, upgrade, quantity-raise, quantity-decrease, the three dispute cases and duplicate-delivery) do change with the order and are pinned there as `ORDER_SENSITIVE`: a later invoice delivered before its first, or a dispute closing before its opening, makes the credit events cite another delivery as their cause.
- **Fix:** hold a cycle invoice for a subscription whose create invoice has not been applied (answer 409 until it is), and cite the invoice's own event as the cause of its credits.

### D8: a refund of an invoice paid before the import books no money (fixed for imports that pass `payments`; open without)

- **Case:** `credits-import-refund` (a bundle outside the matrix: `fixtures/scenarios/credits-import-refund/`), the import that did not record the payment. `credits-import-refund-paid` is the same story with `payments` passed: no discrepancy.
- **Where:** `src/billing/handle.ts` (`BillingRefund`: `ours` is true only when the grants state holds the invoice's `paid` amount, which `handleStripeEvent` sets when it books the invoice's `payment.succeeded`); `grantsStateFromCallerRows` records an imported invoice's credits but not its payment, because the family service booked the payment itself.
- **Why:** "a refund takes back only a payment the books hold". The handler holds none for an invoice it never saw.
- **Impact:** after an import, a refund of an old invoice is found (the index `seedHandlerIndexes` writes names its holder) and takes the credits back, but no `payment.refunded` is booked: refunds, `assets:stripe` and net income are 260.00 off in the case, and `tfd reconcile` prints `missing_in_log refund` and `amount_differs net`. If the family service stops booking money for a school the handler serves (`UPGRADING.md`, "Moving buzz-school's grants onto the handler (D12)", item 2), nobody books it.
- **Fix (tfd 0.11.1, plan §13 item 9):** `grantsStateFromCallerRows` takes `payments` (each pre-import invoice's paid amount, currency and payment refs) and records them on the invoice's record (`paid`, `paymentRefs`), so `ours` is true and the refund is booked like any other, once, with the same checks as for a payment the handler booked (no clamp of an amount above the paid one, as there). An import without `payments` is unchanged, so D8 stays for it: the old logs and the bundle `credits-import-refund` replay as before. The other way out is to keep the family service booking refunds of those invoices.

### What is not a discrepancy but should be known

- **Revenue is booked when paid.** A yearly invoice of 2,600.00 is 2,600.00 of income in the month it is paid, and its 24 visits (12 monthly allowances) are granted at once. That is the documented cash basis (`docs/financial-reports.md#revenue-timing`); the case checks the cash-basis numbers.
- **The month is the school's calendar, not Stripe's.** A payment at 03:00 UTC on 1 February is booked on 31 January in Denver. Stripe's dashboard (UTC) puts it in February. `tfd reconcile` uses the school's calendar for both sides, so it agrees; a person comparing the books with the dashboard by month will see the difference (`month-boundary`).
- **A payout is dated by its arrival** (the `payout.paid` event), Stripe's payout object by its creation. A payout created on 30 January that arrives on 2 February is February's in the books; reconcile pairs them by id (`payout-across-months`), and the balance sheet shows the money in Stripe's balance at the end of January.
- **A refund keeps Stripe's fee**: the fee stays an expense when a payment is refunded, and a fully refunded payment leaves `assets:stripe` at -7.84 (`refund-full`).

## How the oracle was kept honest

Two hand numbers were wrong the first time and were corrected from evidence, not from the code's output: the credits of a yearly invoice (24, not 2: the README says a yearly plan paid once grants twelve monthly allowances per unit), and the dispute fee on a win (Stripe's recorded won dispute returns 26,000 with a fee of 0, so the 15.00 is not given back; the first hand number assumed it was). The fee on each payment is the 2.9% + 30 cents of Stripe's list price, rounded to the cent, and is the number the caller is assumed to pass; the matrix does not test the caller's fee lookup. The dispute and non-USD hand numbers were rewritten after #66 and #68 merged, from the recorded dispute (26,000 withdrawn with a fee of 1,500, 26,000 back with no fee on a win) and the payment above, and the first run on the merged code matched them, so none was adjusted to the output.

## Uncertain

- The dispute balance transactions and the no-fee-back on a win come from test-mode recordings; live rules may differ.
- Whether the `payout.paid` event is made at the payout's arrival (so its date is later than the payout's creation) was taken from Stripe's documentation, not from a live account. A payout that fails and is paid again as a new payout (D3) books as the tests say; whether Stripe makes a new `po_` id for it, and whether it sends `payout.failed` for a payout that was never `paid`, was not checked.
- D2 is latent: no recorded `charge.refunded` lacks `previous_attributes`.
- D4's fix books a refused invoice as soon as it is refused, so a refund of an invoice that stays refused for good is not in the books (see D4). Whether that happens often enough to build the refund side too (`docs/financial-reports.md`, option E) is not known; no real school has been through a refused invoice yet.
- `subject.erased` has no implementation in the Trifold engine (only the event type and its payload schema); the `erasure` case records one after real payments and shows that the money lines do not move and that no personal data (the name and address Stripe's synthetic events carry) is in any shipped file or journal, because the handler never writes it. The rewrite of objects that an erasure means is the family service's and is not run here.
- Nothing here runs against a live account or the family service. The Stripe side is hand-built from documented and recorded shapes. The goldens are hledger 1.52.1's output.
